How to read this

Signed, not asked. These are the rents on bonds actually lodged for new tenancies, not advertised prices. The RTA reports a median per suburb, dwelling type and quarter, and hides cells with too few bonds.

Seasonality. Each quarter's rent is compared with a centred four-quarter moving average, which removes the long-run rise. The bars are the average of those differences across years. "Clear" means at least five years of data and a t-statistic of 2 or more. Suburb quarters with fewer than 10 new bonds are left out. Part of the March premium may be a location effect (more leases signed in dearer inner suburbs when semester starts) rather than every landlord charging more; bond data alone can't separate the two.

Compare like with like. "All dwellings" mixes flats, houses and rooms. In student suburbs many new leases are "Other" dwellings, which can pull the headline median below that of a one-bedroom flat. Pick a dwelling type to compare suburbs fairly.
